Direct Answer • 2026 Virtual Assistant Pricing Standards

For freelance virtual assistants in 2026: A sustainable baseline rate is $32 to $65 per hour for general and executive support, and $65 to $105+ per hour for specialized operations (OBM) and tech VAs. To calculate your exact rate: take your desired net take-home pay, adjust for 25% self-employment taxes, add $4,000–$9,000 for SaaS and office overhead, include a 15–20% business profit margin, and divide across 900–1,100 billable client hours per year (20–24 hours/week).

How Freelance Virtual Assistants Should Price Their Services in 2026

The virtual assistance profession has transformed from routine task execution into high-leverage operational leadership. In 2026, founders, executives, and remote teams gladly pay significant premiums for autonomous operations managers, technical CRM specialists, and executive gatekeepers who eliminate chaos.

"Virtual assistants who bill strictly by the hour cap their income and invite micro-management. When you transition to packaged monthly retainers with guaranteed turnaround SLAs, you stop selling time and start selling operational sanity."

— Melissa Vance, Founder of Elite Executive VA Network & Operations Consultant

VA Rule: Never offer open-ended rollover hours on monthly retainers. Unused hours expire at month-end to protect your schedule capacity and prevent double-booking surges.

The Hidden Overhead of Remote Administrative Support

According to RateCraft's 2026 Global Virtual Assistant & Operations Benchmark, 64.2% of professional VAs spend 12+ hours per week on unbillable client communication, project onboarding, asynchronous task triage, and software tool synchronization.

When you add the cost of Google Workspace, ClickUp, Notion, Slack Pro, Calendly, and high-speed internet to your self-employment tax burden via the IRS Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center, your gross billable requirement is nearly 1.7x your target take-home wage. Compare this against official wage figures from the BLS Administrative Specialists Benchmark and the International Virtual Assistants Association (IVAA).

Frequently Asked Questions for Virtual Assistants (FAQ)

In 2026, general administrative virtual assistants charge between $25 and $40 per hour. Specialized executive assistants charge $45 to $65 per hour, while technical, CRM, and operations managers (OBMs) command $65 to $105+ per hour.

Monthly retainers (e.g. 10, 20, or 40 hours per month) are significantly superior to hourly billing. Retainers provide predictable recurring revenue for the VA and guarantee reserved capacity and priority turnaround for the client. Learn how to structure agreements in our Freelance Retainer Agreements Guide.

A full-time virtual assistant can realistically bill between 20 and 26 hours per week (around 1,000 to 1,200 hours annually). The remaining 14 to 20 hours are consumed by client communication, administrative onboarding, bookkeeping, and business development.

Common tax-deductible expenses include high-speed internet, laptop hardware depreciation, project management software (Asana, ClickUp, Notion), scheduling tools (Calendly), Google Workspace, Zoom Pro, and dedicated home office square footage. Review our full Freelance Taxes & Overhead Deductions Guide.

Professional VA retainer contracts state that hours do not roll over from month to month, protecting your schedule. Any work exceeding the contracted monthly allocation is billed at an agreed overage hourly rate.
