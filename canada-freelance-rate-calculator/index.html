CRA Compliance Essentials: CPP, GST/HST & Incorporation

Transitioning from a T4 permanent employee to a Canadian independent contractor requires navigating three key financial realities:

  • Double Canada Pension Plan (CPP): Salaried employees only see a 5.95% CPP deduction on their pay stubs because their employer matches the other 5.95%. Self-employed individuals must pay the full 11.9% employer + employee amount, capped annually at the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YMPE).
  • The C$30,000 GST/HST Registration Rule: If your gross worldwide revenue exceeds $30,000 over 4 consecutive calendar quarters, you MUST register for a GST/HST account with the CRA. You collect 13% HST in Ontario, 15% in Atlantic provinces, or 5% GST in Alberta. These sales taxes are collected on behalf of the government and do not belong to you, but you can claim Input Tax Credits (ITCs) to refund taxes paid on your software and equipment.
  • Sole Proprietorship vs. Incorporation: While a sole proprietorship is simple to register, incorporating a Federal or Provincial company becomes financially attractive once your earnings exceed C$100k–C$120k. Corporate profits eligible for the Small Business Deduction (SBD) are taxed at just ~9% federally, allowing you to defer personal income tax by retaining capital within the corporation.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How does Canada Pension Plan (CPP) impact self-employed freelance rates?

Unlike standard W-2/T4 employees where the employer pays half of CPP, self-employed Canadian sole proprietors must pay both the employer and employee portions of CPP (totaling 11.9% on net business income between $3,500 and the maximum pensionable earnings limit, plus second-tier CPP2 contributions). This adds thousands in tax obligations that must be built into your hourly rate.

When must Canadian freelancers register for and charge GST/HST?

Under CRA rules, once your worldwide gross taxable revenues exceed $30,000 CAD over any single calendar quarter or four consecutive quarters, you are no longer considered a small supplier and must register for a GST/HST account. You must collect applicable sales tax (5% GST up to 15% HST depending on client province) and can claim Input Tax Credits (ITCs) on business expenses.

When should a Canadian contractor incorporate instead of operating as a Sole Proprietor?

Canadian contractors typically incorporate once net profit consistently exceeds personal living expenses (usually around $100,000–$120,000 CAD net). Incorporating allows you to access the Small Business Deduction (SBD) corporate tax rate (~9% federal plus ~3.2% in Ontario = 12.2% active business rate) and defer personal income taxes on retained corporate earnings.

What is the average freelance contractor day rate in Canada in 2026?

In 2026, Canadian tech contractors, software engineers, and IT specialists bill between C$650 and C$1,200 per day (C$80–C$150/hr). Creative, marketing, and content contractors bill C$450 to C$850 per day (C$55–C$105/hr), while management and enterprise consultants bill C$1,100 to C$2,000+ per day.

Can Canadian freelancers deduct home office expenses on CRA Form T2125?

Yes. Sole proprietors reporting business income on CRA Form T2125 can deduct reasonable home workspace expenses (rent, home insurance, heating, electricity, internet) based on the square footage percentage of the dedicated workspace relative to the home's total area.
