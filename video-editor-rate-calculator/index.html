Video Editor Hourly & Day Rate Calculator

Calculate sustainable hourly, daily, and project fees for video editing. Account for GPU workstation depreciation, DaVinci/Adobe licenses, rendering downtime, and client revisions.

Direct Answer • 2026 Video Editor Pricing Standards

For freelance video editors in 2026: Industry day rates range from $450 to $700 per day for YouTube/social content, and $750 to $1,400+ per day ($85–$160/hr) for commercial, broadcast, and high-end corporate editing. To calculate your minimum viable rate: determine your personal take-home target, factor 25% self-employment taxes, add $10,000–$25,000 in hardware depreciation (Mac Studio/GPU rigs, RAID storage, DaVinci/Adobe/Frame.io licenses), include a 20% business profit reserve, and divide across 900–1,100 billable editing hours per year (20–24 billable hours/week).

How Freelance Video Editors & Motion Designers Should Price in 2026

The video post-production landscape has split into two distinct tiers: commodity micro-editing and high-value commercial finishing. High-growth YouTube creators, agencies, and enterprise marketing teams readily pay premium day rates for editors who understand pacing, sound design, retention curves, and color grading science.

"Video editing is not priced by timeline length; it is priced by audience retention, brand prestige, and commercial storytelling. When you bill by the hour, you are penalizing yourself for learning keyboard shortcuts and efficient conform workflows."

— Julian Thorne, Senior Commercial Finishing Artist & Post Supervisor

Video Editor Rule: Standardize two strict revision rounds with timestamped notes (e.g. Frame.io). Unbounded subjective revisions after picture lock destroy hourly margin faster than any other post-production risk.

The True Studio Overhead of High-End Video Post-Production

According to RateCraft's 2026 Video Post-Production & Hardware Depreciation Survey, 61.4% of professional video editors spend 16+ hours per week unbilled on raw media ingestion, proxy transcoding, client revision triage, render queuing, and cloud backup sync.

Between high-end Apple Silicon or GPU workstations ($4,000–$8,000 replaced every 24–36 months), color-calibrated OLED/Mini-LED reference monitors, RAID and NVMe storage arrays, Adobe Creative Cloud, DaVinci Resolve Studio, Frame.io, commercial sound libraries (Musicbed, Artlist), and high-speed fiber internet, video post-production carries an annual overhead of $12,000 to $25,000. These expenses must be deducted according to the IRS Business Expense Deductions Guide. Compare industry wage baselines with the BLS Film and Video Editors Occupational Outlook, the American Cinema Editors (ACE), and the Motion Picture Editors Guild (IATSE Local 700).

Frequently Asked Questions for Video Editors (FAQ)

In 2026, social media and short-form video editors typically charge $40 to $70 per hour ($350 to $600 per day). Commercial, corporate, and documentary editors charge $75 to $125 per hour ($650 to $1,100 per day), while senior colorists, motion designers, and post-production supervisors charge $120 to $180+ per hour ($1,000 to $1,600+ per day).

For agency, corporate, and commercial productions, day rates (10-hour or 8-hour days) are industry standard because timeline reviews and creative iterations are collaborative. For YouTube creators and short-form brands, flat per-video pricing with strictly defined revision limits and runtime caps yields the highest profitability. Read our Value-Based Pricing vs Hourly Guide.

Video editing requires substantial capital expenditure: Apple Silicon workstations or high-end GPU rigs ($4,000–$8,000 replaced every 2–3 years), RAID and NVMe storage, Adobe CC, DaVinci Studio, Frame.io, and high-speed fiber internet. Editors must build at least $8,000 to $20,000 in annual equipment amortization and overhead into their baseline hourly billing floor. Review our Freelance Taxes & Overhead Guide.

Due to visual fatigue, posture strain, audio monitoring ear-fatigue, and non-billable ingestion, rendering, project archiving, and client reviews, full-time video editors average 20 to 24 billable hours per week (around 50% to 60% of a 40-hour work week).

Standard professional contracts include two rounds of consolidate revisions delivered with timestamped comments (e.g., via Frame.io or ReviewLink). Any structural revisions after picture lock, or requests exceeding two rounds, are billed at the editor's hourly or half-day overage rate.
